# Lumina — Training/Validation Data Provenance

The diligence trail for every dataset touched by the ML pipeline. Keep this
current: "where did your training data come from?" is a due-diligence question,
and "I don't remember" fails it. One row per source, with the license, the
*real* origin (not just the uploader's tag), and the decision + reason.

Principle: a permissive license TAG is not proof of clean provenance. Data
scraped from a third party can't be relicensed by whoever uploaded it. We judge
on origin, not on the badge.

| **`AzharAli05/Resume-Screening-Dataset`** (HF) | MIT | **CONFIRMED synthetic** — rows begin "Here's a professional resume for …" (LLM preamble); all 294 sampled emails are fake `@email.com` | **None** (all fabricated) | 45 roles (case-messy); 9 map cleanly to ours | **Validation only** (independent distribution) | ✅ Inspected & in use |

Notes:
- AzharAli05 is *synthetic*, so it isn't "real" data — but it's generated by a
  DIFFERENT process than our templates, which is exactly what makes it a valid
  independent generalization check. If our template-trained model classifies it
  well, that's real evidence, not leakage.
- Its `Decision` (select/reject) + `Reason` columns are a proxy OUTCOME signal —
  parked for the future outcome-trained ranking model, not used now.
- PII-strip (`ml/pii.py`) runs on it anyway (defense in depth), even if synthetic.

## Rejected (recorded so the decision is auditable)

| `snehaanbhawal/resume-dataset` (Kaggle) / `opensporks/resumes` (HF) | CC0-1.0 | **Invalid CC0.** Card states data was *scraped from livecareer.com* — 2,484 real people's resumes with PII. The uploader can't dedicate strangers' copyrighted, personal work to the public domain. Real PII + dubious rights. |
| Common Crawl | (research access) | Raw web dump; every resume retains original copyright + PII. Access ≠ reuse rights. Extraction effort huge. |
| Internet Archive | (access terms) | Archiving a page grants no reuse rights to the content on it. |
